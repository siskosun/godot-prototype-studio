# Optional prototype experiments

The current mission brief is the source of delivery obligations. Use a prototype-question record only when a design/technical experiment benefits from explicit hypothesis tracking. Do not maintain a second independent acceptance contract.

Define one question whose answer changes an action, evidence that would support or contradict it, a bounded change, and representative scenarios. A pure technical spike needs no invented design pillars. Use observable pillars only when they help resolve competing design choices.

Represent complicated mechanics as trigger, precondition, player action, state change, feedback, and ending. For meaningful recurring choices, identify a plausible reachable context in which each is useful; remove dominated duplicates unless their role is intentional and explained.

Keep machine outcomes separate from human experience. `SUPPORTED`, `REVISE`, `NOT_SUPPORTED`, and `INCONCLUSIVE` describe the narrow hypothesis, not the task's delivery state. An inconclusive fun hypothesis can coexist with a completed runnable prototype when human validation was outside its required acceptance. A failed mandatory mechanic cannot.

The legacy `prototype_contract.md` and validator remain usable for existing research workspaces. Link the mission brief and label overlapping fields as references, not new acceptance. Do not require this template for ordinary feature fixes or every new prototype.
