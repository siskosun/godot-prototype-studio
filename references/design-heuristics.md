# Conditional design heuristics, not universal laws

Use only the subsection relevant to an observed weakness or an unresolved design choice. These are editorial heuristics adapted from the user-supplied mobile-design compilation, not experimentally established guarantees. Do not preload them for unrelated fixes or grade games by how many tips they follow.

Use this pattern: observed issue -> applicable heuristic -> small intervention -> relevant comparison. Keep a change only when it supports the player promise without damaging retained requirements. Do not add a score, fixed quota, approval ceremony or mandatory document.

## Visual identity and style guidance

When assets look individually attractive but incoherent, express the intended experience in a few contrasting words; one to three is a useful starting point, not a required count. Translate each into a project-specific choice about silhouette, palette role, density, material, typography or motion. Example: "busy but legible" may mean a quiet background and a single strong interaction accent; it does not mean every game should be pastel, round or cozy.

Use a representative positive example and a relevant counterexample when adjectives leave a real ambiguity. Images must be supplied, inspected or clearly labeled newly proposed; do not invent approved references. Explain what property is accepted/rejected, not merely "good/bad art". Specify color roles and occupied area when they matter, not arbitrary percentages copied from an article.

Check the representative live scene before freezing detailed production rules. Then reuse the existing visual canon, Theme and first-sample inspection. Do not create a second style guide or repeat the exemplar gate. An approximate measurement becomes a project constraint only when warranted by the actual reference and brief.

Preserve PARTIAL_REFERENCE boundaries. A PIXEL_ACCURATE_REFERENCE target does not authorize replacing its palette/layout with the heuristic's preferred look. If matching conflicts with required legibility, disclose the conflict and resolve the retained choice rather than silently abandoning either requirement.

## Guidance and first-use comprehension

When a player cannot identify an available action, distinguish a hidden affordance, competing visual emphasis, missing rule information and intentional uncertainty. Reduce irrelevant competition, align goal -> action -> consequence, and use scene composition or contextual feedback where appropriate. A visible priority does not require removing simultaneous strategic goals or sandbox freedom.

When a tutorial works only while arrows are present, test the same rule in a comparable new situation with reduced guidance. Fade prompts in response to demonstrated mastery, not an unconditional first/second/third-attempt schedule. Keep help recoverable and preserve accessibility cues. A bot executing the right move is not evidence that a new player understood the rule.

Explicit text, arrows, persistent reminders or user-controlled camera guidance can be better than subtle environmental cues. Do not ban them. Avoid forced camera motion or color-only signals when they impair the intended interaction. Use the target input and actual display, not a beautiful static screenshot, to evaluate the change.

## Short or repeated sessions (only when relevant)

Use when the brief calls for repeat-session play or an observed session ending is unclear or unsatisfying. Being a mobile, round-based or short game alone does not justify persistent progression.

Check whether a round has a comprehensible result and a reasonable stopping point. What remains can be knowledge, practiced skill, a social memory, a created object, a score comparison, or explicit saved progression. Experience points, materials, daily tasks and unlock systems are options, not requirements. Pure competition, finite puzzles and one-off narrative experiences may be complete without them.

When replay matters, examine whether the last attempt gives a concrete new plan or a meaningful variation. Do not add loss compensation, reward popups or a long-term economy merely to hide a weak core. Test the uncertain mechanic without unrelated progression incentives when feasible, while preserving rewards that are themselves causal parts of the mechanic.

Stop and exit should remain clear. A desire to replay must not become a forced retention loop. No prototype check or article example establishes audience retention or monetization uplift.
